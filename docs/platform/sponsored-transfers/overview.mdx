A sponsored transfer moves a stablecoin from an address your user controls to any recipient, without the user holding the chain's native gas token. Bitnob pays the network fee and recovers it as a small fee in the token being sent. The user never needs SOL, ETH, or TRX, and never has to buy some first.

The sender is **external**: they hold their own key, in their own wallet, and have no Bitnob wallet. Bitnob never holds the funds or the key. The user signs a payload you show them, and Bitnob adds its own signature for the gas and broadcasts.

## When to use it

- A self-custody wallet app whose users hold USDT on Tron but no TRX.
- A checkout or payout flow where customers pay in USDC from their own wallet, and you don't want "insufficient gas" to be a failure mode.
- A product that charges its own fee per transfer, on top of the network cost. See [Charge a partner fee](/docs/platform/sponsored-transfers/charge-a-partner-fee).

## Which gas tool to use

Bitnob Enterprise has three ways to take gas off your users' hands. They fit different custody setups.

| You want to | Use | Who holds the key |
|---|---|---|
| Send from **your users' own addresses**, with the fee taken in the token | Sponsored transfers (this section) | The end user |
| Pay gas for transfers out of **your Bitnob Enterprise wallets** | A gas station, then `sponsor: true` on [Create transaction](/api-reference/platform/transactions/create-transaction) | Bitnob Secure Enclave, governed by your policies |
| Broadcast a Tron transaction **someone already signed**, with your gas station paying | [Sponsor and broadcast](/api-reference/platform/gasstations/sponsor-and-broadcast-external-tron-transaction) | Whoever signed it |

With sponsored transfers you don't pre-fund anything. With a gas station, you pre-fund native gas yourself and pay it.

## Supported chains and assets

| Chain | `chain` value | Signing scheme |
|---|---|---|
| Solana | `solana` | `solana_ed25519` |
| Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum, Optimism, Polygon, BSC | `ethereum`, `base`, `arbitrum`, `optimism`, `polygon`, `bsc` | `evm_7702` |
| Tron | `tron` | `tron_router` |

The asset is a supported stablecoin on that chain, typically USDC on Solana and the EVM chains, and USDT on Tron. [List assets](/api-reference/platform/assets/list-assets) shows what your organisation can use. An unsupported chain or asset returns `400`.

The network follows your API key. A live key uses mainnet, and a sandbox key uses the chain's testnet.

## How it works

<Steps>
  <Step title="Quote">
    Call [Create sponsored transfer](/api-reference/platform/sponsored-transfers/create-sponsored-transfer-quote) with the chain, asset, sender, recipient, and amount. You get back the fee, the total the sender is debited, a `sign` payload, and an `expires_at`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The user signs">
    Show the user the amounts, and have them sign the `sign` payload exactly as returned, with the sender's key. What to sign depends on the chain. See [Signing by chain](/docs/platform/sponsored-transfers/signing-by-chain).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit">
    Send the signature to [Submit signatures](/api-reference/platform/sponsored-transfers/submit-signatures) before `expires_at`. Bitnob checks it, adds its own signature, and broadcasts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Track">
    Follow the transfer to `completed` with `sponsored_transfer.*` webhooks or [Get sponsored transfer](/api-reference/platform/sponsored-transfers/get-sponsored-transfer).
  </Step>
</Steps>

[Send your first sponsored transfer](/docs/platform/sponsored-transfers/send-your-first-sponsored-transfer) walks through this end to end on Solana.
