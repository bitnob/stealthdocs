A policy is the set of rules your organisation enforces on every signing request. The Bitnob Secure Enclave evaluates the policy before it produces a signature, so a rule that blocks a transfer blocks it in hardware, not in your application code. An API key can only move funds the policy allows.

Your organisation enforces one policy at a time. That policy holds an ordered list of rules. Each rule has conditions, such as an amount above a threshold or a destination outside a set of addresses, and an action to take when every condition matches.

## What a policy can do

| Action | What happens to the transaction |
|---|---|
| `allow` | It is signed and broadcast. |
| `block` | It is rejected before signing. Nothing is broadcast. |
| `require_approval` | It is held in `pending_authorization` until enough approvers approve it. |
| `delay` | It is held in `delayed` until a time lock expires, then executes on its own. |
| `alert` | It proceeds, and a `policy.alert` event informs the rule's notify targets. |

The first rule whose conditions all match decides the outcome. [How policy evaluation works](/docs/platform/policies/how-policy-evaluation-works) covers the order, the conditions, and what happens when nothing matches.

## Why policies are safe to rely on

- **No single key can change them.** Activating a policy always goes through your organisation's quorum, which needs at least two approvers. An API key can draft a policy but never enforce one by itself. See [Policy lifecycle and quorum](/docs/platform/policies/policy-lifecycle-and-quorum).
- **The enforced policy is immutable.** You change it by drafting a replacement and activating that, so every change is reviewed and recorded.
- **You can test before you enforce.** [Dry-run evaluation](/docs/platform/policies/test-rules-with-dry-run-evaluation) runs any draft against a sample request and returns a per-rule trace, with no side effects.
- **Every change is audited.** Each policy carries an audit log of who created, edited, and activated it.

## How you work with policies

<Steps>
  <Step title="Discover what you can write">
    [Rule reference](/docs/platform/policies/rule-reference) lists the categories, actions, condition fields, and operators. [List approver candidates](/api-reference/platform/users/list-approver-candidates) lists who can approve.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Draft and lint">
    Create a draft with [Create draft policy](/api-reference/platform/policies/create-draft-policy), then check it with [Validate stored draft policy](/api-reference/platform/policies/validate-stored-draft-policy). Drafts are never enforced.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Test against real scenarios">
    Run sample transfers through [Dry-run policy evaluation](/api-reference/platform/policies/dry-run-policy-evaluation) with the draft's `policy_id`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Activate through quorum">
    [Activate policy](/api-reference/platform/policies/activate-policy) stages the draft. It becomes `enforced` once your quorum approvers approve it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

[Write and activate your first policy](/docs/platform/policies/write-and-activate-your-first-policy) walks through all four steps with working requests.
