Once money moves, the question changes from "does this work" to "what happened to this specific payment". Your system has to answer that without a call to support.

This guide covers what to record and how to connect the records so that one query returns the whole story.

## The three identifiers

Every trace hangs on three values. Record all three, on every step.

| Identifier | Who creates it | What it answers |
|---|---|---|
| `reference` | You, before the first attempt | Which business action is this? |
| `correlation_id` | Bitnob, per request | Which exact API call was this? |
| `event_id` | Bitnob, per webhook | Which delivery was this? |

The `reference` ties your record to ours. The `correlation_id` appears in every error body and identifies one request in our systems. The `event_id` distinguishes a first delivery from a retry.

<Warning>
  A log line with a message but no identifier is close to useless during an incident. Attach the reference to every line you write about a transaction.
</Warning>

## What to record

Log five things:

- Every request you send, with its reference and the response status.
- Every webhook you receive, with its event name, event id, and full payload.
- Every quote you create, with its rate and `expires_at`.
- Every error, with its `code` and `correlation_id`.
- Every state change, appended rather than overwritten.

```json
{
  "reference": "payout_20260408_a41f9c",
  "type": "payout",
  "status": "initiated",
  "quote_id": "QT2_21015643",
  "from_asset": "USDT",
  "to_currency": "NGN",
  "amount": "0.724761",
  "settlement_amount": "1000",
  "expires_at": "2026-07-04T22:12:36Z",
  "correlation_id": "req_019d9b7e-9e76-711e-afbe-aba9b9455412",
  "created_at": "2026-07-04T21:56:36Z"
}
```

### Append, do not overwrite

Store status changes as new rows, not as an updated field.

| reference | status | source | at |
|---|---|---|---|
| `payout_20260408_a41f9c` | initiated | api | 2026-04-08T12:45:00Z |
| `payout_20260408_a41f9c` | processing | webhook | 2026-04-08T12:45:41Z |
| `payout_20260408_a41f9c` | delivered | webhook | 2026-04-08T12:47:12Z |

An overwritten field tells you where a payment is. An append-only log tells you how it got there and how long each stage took. Only the second answers a dispute.

## Redact before you log

Never write these to a log, a dashboard, or an error tracker:

- Your `CLIENT_SECRET`.
- The `X-Auth-Signature` header.
- Full card numbers or card security details.

Log the `CLIENT_ID` and the `correlation_id` instead. Both identify the caller without exposing the credential. See [API keys and credential security](/docs/getting-started/api-best-practices/api-keys-and-credentials).

## Trace one transaction end to end

![Transaction Flow](/images/transaction-flow.svg)

A complete trace covers each step:

1. The user's request into your system.
2. The quote you created, and the rate you displayed.
3. The request you sent to Bitnob, and its response status.
4. Each webhook you received, and whether you processed it.
5. The final state, and when you told the user.

Server-side, resolve a single record with [Get transaction by id or reference](/api-reference/transactions/get-transaction-by-id). Sweep a period with [Get all transactions](/api-reference/transactions/list-transactions).

## Reconcile on a schedule

Webhook retries are finite. An endpoint that is down long enough misses events permanently, and no amount of logging recovers what you never received.

Run a scheduled job that compares our record against yours:

<Steps>
  <Step title="List recent transactions">
    Query [Get all transactions](/api-reference/transactions/list-transactions) for the period since your last run.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Match on reference">
    Join our records to yours by reference.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Flag the mismatches">
    Anything complete on our side and pending on yours is a missed delivery. Anything pending on our side past its expected window needs a human.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Repair and record">
    Apply the correct state, and log that reconciliation made the change rather than a webhook.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Alert on the right signals

Alert on conditions that mean money is stuck, not on raw error counts.

| Alert | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Webhook processing failures rise | Your handler is broken; events are being lost |
| Payout failure rate crosses your baseline | A corridor or a provider is degraded |
| Quotes expiring before execution | Your flow is too slow, or the interface is confusing |
| Reconciliation finds mismatches | Deliveries are being missed |
| Requests failing with `401` | A credential is wrong or expired |

Send them somewhere a person reads. A message to a chat channel beats a dashboard nobody opens.

Metrics tools such as Datadog, Prometheus, or Grafana handle the counting. Log tools such as CloudWatch or Logtail handle the search. Either is enough to start.

## Build an internal lookup tool

Support questions arrive as "where is my money", not as a reference. Build one internal page that lets your team:

- Search by reference, customer, or amount.
- See the full status history.
- See the raw request and response.
- Replay a failed webhook.
- Mark a case resolved, with a note.

This is usually the highest-leverage internal tool a payments integration has, and it takes a day to build.

## Show users their own trace

Expose a reduced version to the user: the status, the amount they sent, the rate they got, the timestamps, and what happens next if it is stuck.

Most support tickets are requests for information the user could have read themselves.
