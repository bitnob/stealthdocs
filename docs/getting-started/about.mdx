Bitnob was founded on the belief that financial services should be accessible regardless of location. The name references the genesis block, the first block in the Bitcoin chain. Our roots are in African markets, and the infrastructure is open to developers and businesses anywhere.

The goal is to bring crypto and payment infrastructure to Africa without compromising on global standards, and to shorten the time between an idea and a first transaction.

## The problem

Businesses in emerging markets that want to adopt crypto or modern payment rails face high costs and steep technical barriers. This hits startups hardest, since they rarely have the capital or in-house expertise to integrate financial services from the ground up. Established providers tend to overlook local currency handling and country-level regulatory requirements, which makes it hard to build products that fit African contexts.

The demand is not only local. Companies worldwide need to reach customers, suppliers, and markets in Africa. The platforms that serve them are usually either narrow in scope or too expensive for smaller players to onboard. Bitnob exists to reduce that friction while keeping African payment rails central rather than an afterthought.

## Who uses it

The infrastructure supports remittance applications, fintech platforms operating across African countries, and businesses that need to accept, store, and distribute digital assets or handle local currency payments.

Common integrations include payment gateways building crypto-enabled checkout, payroll services paying out in digital assets, and B2B platforms bridging distant markets.
