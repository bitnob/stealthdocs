Outbound transfers go through one endpoint regardless of chain. You name the destination, amount, token, and chain; Bitnob broadcasts, tracks confirmations, and reports the outcome as a webhook.

The minimum send is \$1. Sends draw on your balance, checked with [List balances](/api-reference/balances/list-balances), and network fees are deducted from it too.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Validate the destination">
    Run [Validate address](/api-reference/addresses/validate-address) on any user-supplied address before submitting. It catches malformed addresses and chain mismatches, the one error class that is unrecoverable after broadcast.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit the withdrawal">
    Full schema: [Initiate withdrawal](/api-reference/withdrawals/initiate-withdrawal).

    ```http
    POST /api/withdrawals
    ```

    ```json
    {
      "to_address": "TRMPscTMrXg21YuDFuvpXRUkwEbipHEZXN",
      "amount": "2000000",
      "currency": "USDT",
      "chain": "tron",
      "reference": "testing_012",
      "description": "Vendor settlement"
    }
    ```

    <ParamField body="to_address" type="string" required>
      Recipient's on-chain address.
    </ParamField>

    <ParamField body="amount" type="string" required>
      Amount in the token's smallest unit, 6 decimals: `"2000000"` is 2 USDT.
    </ParamField>

    <ParamField body="currency" type="string" required>
      `USDT` or `USDC`.
    </ParamField>

    <ParamField body="chain" type="string" required>
      Network to send over, for example `tron`, `ethereum`, `solana`. Sendable chains per token come from [Supported chains](/api-reference/addresses/supported-chains).
    </ParamField>

    <ParamField body="reference" type="string" required>
      Your unique identifier, and the idempotency key: the same reference never sends twice.
    </ParamField>

    <ParamField body="description" type="string">
      Free-text note for your records.
    </ParamField>

    The response returns the transfer in a pending state with fees visible in `centFees`. Pending is expected: broadcasting and confirmation happen asynchronously.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm from the webhook">
    `transfer.success` fires once the chain's confirmation threshold is met; `transfer.failed` if the transfer is rejected, reverted, or times out, with held funds returned to your balance. Act on the events rather than polling; [Get transaction by id](/api-reference/transactions/get-transaction-by-id) resolves a reference if you need to re-check one. See [Receive stablecoin webhooks](/docs/stablecoins/webhooks) for payloads and verification.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Sending on stellar

Forward any memo the destination gives you exactly as provided; exchanges routinely require one, and a missing memo strands the funds at the destination's operations desk. Details under [Initiate withdrawal](/api-reference/withdrawals/initiate-withdrawal).

## Keep fees legible

Fees are dynamic per chain and visible per send in `centFees`. Two habits keep users happy: prefer low-fee chains, such as Tron or Polygon, for small amounts, and show the arithmetic in your UI: sending \$1.00, network fee \$0.01, recipient gets \$0.99.

For transfers between wallets on your own Bitnob account, skip the chain entirely: [internal transfers](/docs/stablecoins/internal-wallet-to-wallet-transfers) settle instantly with no gas.
